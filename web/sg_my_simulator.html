    Where the default numbers come from

    Inputs marked Count are published figures you can check: personnel and reservists from the IISS Military Balance 2024, aircraft and ships from the IISS and the two forces' public fleet lists. Inputs marked Estimate can't be counted, so each shows the reasoning behind its default. The model turns counts into power like this: fighting troops are 75% of active personnel; reserves are reservists × the ready share; air power is combat aircraft × the share ready to fly; naval power is 5 per frigate, 4 per submarine and 2 per corvette or missile patrol ship. For scale, Singapore spends about US$13 billion a year on defence and Malaysia about US$4 billion (SIPRI, 2023–24). New deliveries, such as Singapore's F-35Bs and Malaysia's FA-50s and LCS frigates, are left out until they enter service; add them with the sliders.

    How the model works

    Each day, both sides' combat power is troops × equipment × training × morale × supply × air and sea control × command and surveillance × a bonus for fighting on home ground. Losses follow a noisy Lanchester square law, and the front moves toward the weaker side. Reservists arrive after the mobilisation delay. Losses and lost ground wear down morale. A side loses when its core territory falls (the front passes its strategic depth), its army breaks, or its morale collapses. Otherwise the war ends undecided at a ceasefire or after the maximum length.

    The uncertainty slider spreads every number you set by that much in each run, so no two runs are the same. That spread is what lets the page tell which assumptions matter. Default numbers are rough public estimates (IISS Military Balance, SIPRI, public fleet listings). The air and naval indices compare the two sides; their absolute values mean nothing.

    Day-by-day replay

    Pick one of the simulated wars and watch it unfold. The line is the front: shading shows who holds the ground. Dashed lines mark each side's core, set by the strategic-depth sliders. Coastlines, roads and built-up areas are real map data. The front, unit markers and arrows are abstract: they are drawn from each side's simulated strength in six sectors, not real unit locations.

    Day 0

    Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag or shift-drag to pan. Double-click anywhere on the ground to stand there and look at the front; then drag to look around and use W A S D or the arrow keys to walk. Height is exaggerated 6 times so hills and the front wall stand out.
